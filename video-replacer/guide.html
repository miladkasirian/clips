Course Video Replacer

You record a lecture in Persian or English. It comes back in an American voice, still in step with the picture, with subtitles in both languages. Nothing about the video is re-encoded and the video itself never leaves this computer.

What this is

One folder on your machine. Drag an .mp4 onto Replace.bat and wait.

your lecture.mp4
  → your lecture.en.mp4 the same video, your voice replaced
  → your lecture.original.srt subtitles in the language you spoke
  → your lecture.english.srt subtitles in English, same timings
  → your lecture.report.txt every line that had to be squeezed

The rule this is built around Everything that touches the video is ffmpeg, not a model. A 500MB file is never uploaded and never re-encoded. Only the sound leaves, as a 32 kbps mono mp3 — about 14MB for an hour.

The app

Double-click Course Video Replacer.exe. That is the whole thing — there is nothing else to run, nothing to install first, and nothing to edit by hand.

TabWhat is in it
ConvertThe video, where the results go, whether to stop and show you the English first, and how much a line may be sped up. Then Start.
VoiceEvery voice you can use — the eleven ready-made ones and any you have added. Hear it plays a sample. Add a voice… makes a new one from a recording.
Words to keep rightThe glossary, edited in place. Save and it applies to the next run.
SettingsYour OpenAI key, ffmpeg, the local voice, and the models. All of it from inside the app.

The first time

  1. Open Settings, paste your OpenAI key, press Save. It goes into key.txt beside the exe and never leaves this computer.
  2. If ffmpeg says missing, press Get it. The app downloads and unpacks it itself.
  3. Only if you want a voice of your own: press Install next to The local voice.
Why the voice model is not inside the exe The exe is 11MB. The cloned voice needs several gigabytes of PyTorch, which has no business inside an executable and would make it slow to start and impossible to update. So the app installs it beside itself, into .venv\, and drives it as a separate process that loads the model once and stays running. Everything else — the window, the whole pipeline, ffmpeg, the OpenAI calls — is in the exe.

Remember these settings, next to Start, writes what is on screen into config.json, so the next time you open the app it is already the way you left it.

The panel that opens before it speaks

If Show me every line first is ticked, a window opens partway through with one card per line: the moment it covers, how many words fit there, what you said, and — in a box you can type into — the English that will be spoken over it.

Fix whatever is wrong and press Approve and make the video. Press Cancel and nothing further happens.

This is the cheap moment Nothing has been spoken when that panel opens, and nothing has been spent on the voice. A name the translator got wrong costs nothing to fix here and a whole run to fix afterwards.

Emptying a box does not delete that line — it keeps what it had. Deleting a piece of your lecture by accidentally clearing a field is not a thing that should be possible.

There is still a console way python replacer.py "your.mp4" does the same job with a text file instead of a window, and --go skips the stop. Both call the same convert(), so a fix to one is a fix to both. You never need it; it is there because a pipeline that can only be run from a window cannot be tested.

Running one

Choose the video on the Convert tab and press Start. Progress appears in the box underneath. A two-minute lecture takes about a minute; an hour takes roughly twenty — longer with a cloned voice.

Start small The first time, use a two or three minute recording. It costs a few cents and finishes while you watch, and you find out whether the voice and the phrasing are what you want before you commit an hour of lecture to it.

Checking it before it speaks

A run stops in the middle, on purpose. It writes output\<name>.review.txt and opens it:

[002]  00:08 - 00:17   (max 22 words)
  FA: گذینه لولیست داریم بعد گذینه کلیپ داریم اسپین داریم
  EN: We have the Lolist option, then the clip option, Spin, etc.

Every line you said, and under it the English that will be spoken over that exact moment of video. Change any EN: line, save the file, and run Approve.bat.

Why here and not afterwards The voice is the expensive step and the one that cannot be un-said. A word the translator got wrong is free to fix at this point and costs a whole run to fix after. Nothing has been spoken when the sheet appears, and nothing has been spent on the voice.

The (max 22 words) is what will fit in that moment. Going over it is allowed — that line is then sped up, and if it still will not fit it runs over and the report says so.

To skip the stop entirely, set "review": false in config.json, or run python replacer.py "your.mp4" --go.

Words it gets wrong

Some mistakes are not one-offs. A name you invented has no translation: say MLAD out loud in Persian and the transcriber writes ملاد, and the writer turns that into "Milad" — a different thing, in every line, in every video.

Put it in glossary.txt, one per line:

ملاد = MLAD
اسپین = Spin
کانتنت = Content

Those are handed to the writer as terms it must use exactly as written. The review sheet is for the mistake that happened once; the glossary is for the one that would keep happening.

What comes out

FileWhat it is
.en.mp4Your video with the English audio. The picture is the identical stream that went in.
.original.srtWhat you actually said, in the language you said it, with real timings.
.english.srtThe English, on exactly the same timings, so the two line up.
.report.txtWhich lines were tight, and which could not fit at all.

Reading the report

Two different things can go wrong with a line, and the report keeps them apart on purpose:

What it saysWhat it means
too long for its 2.0s fix this The English for that moment genuinely needs more seconds than the moment has. Say less there and run it again.
pushed 1.4s late a symptom This line is fine. An earlier one ran over and shoved it along. Fix the earlier one and this goes away by itself.
sped up to 109% fine Nothing to do. Nine percent is not audible.

An earlier version called all three of these the same thing, and one bad sentence turned into twenty-three lines of blame. If you only ever read one number, read "Lines whose English is too long for their moment". When that is zero, the run is good.

Choosing the voice

Run python voices.py. It writes a ten-second sample of every voice into samples\ — all of them the same price — and measures how much dead air the voice is adding while it is there.

Play them, then put the one you want in config.json:

"voice": "ash"

The eleven: onyx ash echo ballad verse sage alloy fable coral nova shimmer.

config.json

SettingDefaultWhat it does
voiceonyxWhich voice speaks. See above.

min_tempo0.90How much a very short line may be slowed to fill its moment.

join_gap0.8A pause longer than this is left as a break between lines.
chunk_minutes12How long each piece sent to the transcriber is. Boundaries land on silence.

transcribewhisper-1Do not change this one — see below.

Why whisper-1 and not something newer gpt-4o-transcribe is the newer model, and it cannot be used here: verbose_json and timestamps are whisper-1 only — the newer ones return plain json with no times in it. Without timestamps there is nothing to line the English up against.

The six steps

StepWhere it happens
1. take the sound outffmpeg, here. Mono, 16 kHz, 32 kbps.
2. write down what you saidOpenAI whisper-1, in pieces cut at silences
3. turn it into EnglishOpenAI gpt-4o, with a word budget per line
4. speak it and fit itOpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts, then ffmpeg here
5. put it back on the videoffmpeg, here. -c:v copy.
6. write the subtitleshere

Step 5 is why size does not matter. -c:v copy is a stream copy: the picture is moved across untouched, so a 500MB file takes about as long as a 5MB one and the quality is bit-for-bit what you recorded.

Fitting English to your picture

English is never the same length as the Persian it came from. The picture is never altered, so the sound has to give — in this order:

  1. Say it at a natural speed.
  2. If it will not fit, speed it up — never past max_tempo. atempo changes speed without changing pitch, so it still sounds like the same person rather than a chipmunk.
  3. If it still will not fit, use the silence that follows it.
  4. If it still will not fit, let it run over and name it in the report. Better you know and shorten that sentence than the voice becomes unlistenable.

Why sentences, not fragments

This is the change that made the difference, and it came out of the first real lecture.

The transcriber hands back short pieces — a real two-minute lecture came back as 37 of them, some two seconds long. Two seconds holds about five spoken words. So:

Line 5 — a 2.5 second moment

"When I select Spin here, the videos start playing" — nine words. It needed 3.6 words a second. The voice speaks at 2.6, and 3.3 at the very limit. It could not fit however it was written.

Line 7 — the very next moment, 2 seconds

"And there," — two words, with a second and a half doing nothing.

Neither line can lend room to the other while they are separate lines. Joined into one sentence, the pair has room to spare. And once a line ran over, every line after it was pushed along, so one impossible sentence became a page of failures.

So now: fragments a breath apart are joined into sentences, and each line is handed to the writer with the number of words its moment will actually hold:

3. [max 7 words] بعد میرم توی قسمت ریویو کلیپس

Telling the model "shorter is better" was a wish. Telling it "seven words" is a constraint.

Why not YouTube

The obvious idea — upload the video, let YouTube transcribe it, take the captions, delete the video. It does not work, for four separate reasons:

  • An upload costs 1600 quota units. The daily allowance is 10,000, so six videos a day.
  • captions.download needs OAuth, not the API key you already have.
  • It only works for a video you own, and is widely reported to refuse the automatic captions — which are the only ones a fresh upload has.
  • It means waiting on a queue you do not control, and then remembering to delete a private lecture from the internet.

Taking the audio out here is faster, predictable, cheaper, gives real timestamps, and leaves nothing anywhere that needs cleaning up.

Using a voice of your own

In the Voice tab, press Add a voice…, choose any recording of someone speaking — an mp4, an m4a, anything ffmpeg reads — and give it a name. It appears in the list straight away and can be picked like any other voice.

What happens to the recording: the sound is taken out, made mono, the silence trimmed, and capped at forty seconds, into voice\<name>.wav. Past forty seconds the model gains nothing and only gets slower.

Give it a recording in English Not Persian. The model copies the timbre of whatever it is given, and giving it the language it has to speak gives it far more to work with. Both were made and compared — _your-cloned-voice.mp3 against _cloned-from-persian.mp3 in samples\ — and the difference is audible.

The first time a cloned voice is used the model downloads once, about 1.8GB. It needs Setup-voice.bat run once first; the ready-made voices need none of this.

The graphics card, and why it was not being used

It was installed on the processor and quietly stayed there. The reason is a trap worth knowing: pip install torch on Windows gives you the CPU build. Nothing warns you, nothing fails — it simply runs several times slower for ever. The CUDA build is on a separate index and has to be asked for by name.

The tick box Use the graphics card next to Install asks for it. Measured on this machine, same voice, same sentence:

SpeedAn hour of lecture
processor2.3× real timeover two hours
graphics card (RTX 3050, 4GB)0.7× real timeabout forty minutes

The Settings tab does not just say "installed" — it asks the installed torch which device it will actually use and reports that, because "installed" is exactly the word that hid this for a whole session.

If the card has too little memory for the model, the voice moves to the processor by itself, mid-lecture if it has to, and says so in the log rather than losing the run. XTTS-v2 is licensed for non-commercial use.

Why it had to be local

The obvious question: it has an hour of your voice — can it just sound like you?

Not through OpenAI, on this account. Custom voices do exist in the API now, and the documentation says they are limited to eligible customers without saying who. Asking is the only honest way to find out, and probe-voice.py asks — it makes four read-only requests and creates nothing:

audio/voices           no such endpoint on this account
audio/voice_consents   no such endpoint on this account
voices                 no such endpoint on this account
voice_consents         no such endpoint on this account

Four 404s. Not "denied" — the endpoints are not there at all for this account. So the eleven preset voices are what there is, through OpenAI.

So it was done locally instead, and it works

XTTS-v2 runs on this machine, clones from a sample of you, and costs nothing to run. It is installed in .venv\ beside the app — its own environment on purpose, because installing it into the main Python broke a package that was already there. Nothing outside that folder is touched.

How much of a spoken line is silence

"And that's it." — three words — came back as a 1.42 second file, of which 0.70 seconds was silence. Half. Over a lecture that is half a minute of dead air pushing every later line out of step. It is trimmed off now.

Bugs found and fixed

The English fell behind, and it was not because you spoke fast

That was the first guess and it was wrong. Three-word lines were overrunning two-second slots, which cannot be a length problem. It was fragments too short to hold a sentence, and a report that blamed every line downstream of the real culprit. See Why sentences.

The console died on the first Persian word

'charmap' codec can't encode character 'ی' — the Windows console is cp1252, and printing the transcript killed the whole run. An hour of work is not worth losing to a print statement: the output is UTF-8 now, and anything the console cannot draw is replaced rather than fatal.

ffmpeg was installed and still "not found"

winget adds itself to the path of windows opened afterwards. The one you ran Setup in was opened before. It now also looks in winget's own package folder, so the install works immediately.

When something goes wrong

It saysDo this
No OpenAI keyMake key.txt in this folder, the key on one line, nothing else.
ffmpeg is not installedRun Setup.bat once.
HTTP 401 from audio/transcriptionsThe key is wrong or has been revoked. Make a new one.
HTTP 429Out of credit, or too many requests. Check the OpenAI billing page.
it stopped half waySet keep_work: true and run it again — it will not pay twice for the transcription or the English.
lots of "too long" linesLower longest_line is not the answer — raise it. Longer sentences have more room to move.

The test

python test-pipeline.py. It builds a made-up 40-second lecture, runs the whole thing on it, and checks nineteen things. The three steps that cost money are replaced by stubs, so it spends nothing and needs no key.

What it actually proves, which is hard to see by watching the video once:

  • every line lands on the moment of video it belongs to — by measuring the energy in the finished audio half-second by half-second, not by trusting the code;
  • the gaps between lines are still silent;
  • the picture came out as the same stream that went in;
  • both subtitle files line up with each other and with the video;
  • a line too long for its moment is capped and named, and the lines it merely pushed are counted separately;
  • fragments a breath apart become one sentence, and a real pause stays a break;
  • the silence either side of a spoken line is actually cut off.
