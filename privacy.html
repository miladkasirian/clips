Privacy Policy

Course Video Replacer — a desktop application used by one person to put an English voice on his own recorded university lectures.

Last updated: 30 August 2026

What this application is

Course Video Replacer runs entirely on the computer it is installed on. There is no website behind it, no account to create, no server that belongs to it, and no database. It is used by its author to prepare teaching material for his own courses.

Nothing is collected This application has no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no advertising. Its author receives no information about how, when, or whether it is used.

What it does with your YouTube account

If — and only if — you choose to sign in to YouTube inside the application, it asks Google for the scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.force-ssl. It uses that permission for exactly two read-only calls, against a video you already own:

  • captions.list — to see which subtitle tracks a video of yours has.
  • captions.download — to read the text of one of those tracks, so the lecture can be translated and re-voiced without transcribing the audio again.
What it never does It does not upload videos. It does not create, edit, or delete videos, captions, playlists, comments, subscriptions or any other part of your channel. It does not read anything about other people's channels. It makes no write request of any kind to YouTube.

The permission Google grants is broader than what the application uses, because youtube.force-ssl is the narrowest scope Google offers that allows reading a caption track at all. The application's source code is public and can be read at github.com/miladkasirian/clips.

Where your information is kept

WhatWhere it is stored
Your Google sign-in token In a file named youtube_token.json, in the application's own folder on your computer. It is never transmitted anywhere except to Google, to renew itself.
Your OAuth client file client_secret.json, in the same folder. It comes from your own Google Cloud project and never leaves the computer.
The caption text Written into subtitle files beside the finished video, on your computer.
Work in progress A work folder, emptied when the application is closed.

None of these files are transmitted to the author of the application or to anyone else. There is nowhere for them to be transmitted to.

The one third party

When the application translates a lecture and speaks it, it sends text — and, when you have not supplied a YouTube transcript, the extracted audio — to OpenAI, using an API key that you supply yourself and that is billed to your own account. Their handling of it is governed by OpenAI's privacy policy. The video file itself is never sent to OpenAI or to anywhere else; it is only ever read and written on your own computer.

Taking the permission back

You can revoke this application's access to your Google account at any time, without asking anyone, at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Deleting youtube_token.json from the application's folder also signs it out. Neither action leaves anything behind elsewhere, because there is nowhere else.

Children

This application is a tool for preparing university teaching material. It is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone.

Changes

If what the application does with your data ever changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top changes too.
